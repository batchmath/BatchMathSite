# v13.0.A: experimental interactive 3D cross sections (Beta)

This is the first experiment for BatchMath Version 13. It is on branch `claude/v13.0a-cross-sections-3d`, created from
`main` at `9dbb4f841c6b4e6300c640e1b83b2dad82df1521` (the v12.9.G integrated release). It is not production: `main` is
unchanged and nothing is deployed.

## The supported problem type

The Volume by Cross Sections engine (`assets/unit6-volume.js`) has three families:
- `cs_basic`: non-calculator, find the volume, or A(x) first;
- `cs_setup`: non-calculator, write the integral, including regions that need two integrals;
- `cs_calc`: calculator.

Each family chooses a cross-section shape (square, semicircle, quarter circle, equilateral triangle, two isosceles
right triangles, two rectangles) and a direction (perpendicular to the x-axis or to the y-axis).

**Supported: square cross sections perpendicular to the x-axis, with the base bounded by functions of x, in
Volume by Cross Sections, Non-Calculator Practice.** These are the problems from `cs_basic` and `cs_setup` whose shape
is `square` and whose slices are on x. They cover every region these families use:
- caps y = c − kx²;
- the half cap;
- the semicircle and the whole disk;
- the triangle;
- pairs of power curves;
- √x and power curves with a vertical line;
- y = H with y = xᵖ;
- y = eˣ with y = 1;
- the two-piece region y = c√x, y = x − k, y = 0, where the lower boundary changes.

Everything else keeps its present behaviour and shows no 3D button:
- every other shape;
- slices perpendicular to the y-axis;
- the calculator page;
- the reviews and all other pages.

On calculator problems the region is shown only after Part A, because students are meant to graph it on their own
calculators. A model in the question would give that away, so the calculator page is not supported.

## What a student sees

- **The button.** Under the question, after the 2D "Show Graph" control, a supported question has a button styled
  like it: **Show 3D Model (Beta)**. The model is closed at first. Opening it changes the button to **Hide 3D Model**;
  pressing it again closes the model. The 2D graph stays where it was and works as before.
- **The model.** The base region (gold, like the 2D shading) lies in the xy-plane. The solid stands on it (translucent
  blue), with the edges along its top and nine square outlines along it, showing how the squares stack. One cross
  section is highlighted in yellow at the same x as the slice in the 2D picture, with both of its sides labelled s.
  The x, y and z axes are drawn and labelled, over a light grid.
- **Scale.** The model is drawn at one scale on every axis, so a square looks square.
- **Controls.** Mouse: drag to rotate, scroll to zoom. Touch: one finger rotates, two fingers pinch to zoom. Reset
  view returns to the starting view.
- **Nothing is given away.** No volume, integral or formula for A(x) is shown.

## How it is built

**Data from the generator.** `famCsBasic` and `famCsSetup` keep the generator's own region on the problem as a
non-enumerable `u6Solid` property. It holds:
- each piece's interval;
- its bottom and top boundary as the generator's own functions of x;
- the shape and direction;
- the slice position the 2D picture uses.

Nothing random is drawn for it, and nothing visible or stored changes. 2,070 problems compared seed by seed with
`main` are identical in question, prompts, answers, grading data and explanations.

**`assets/unit6-3d/solid-geometry.mjs` (pure, tested in Node).** It builds the solid from those functions:
- The side of the square at x is s(x) = top(x) − bot(x), used for both its width (y from bot to top) and its height
  (z from 0 to s).
- Each piece is sampled with cosine spacing, which puts more samples near the ends where boundaries such as
  √(r² − x²) are steep. Every breakpoint between pieces is a sample, so the change of boundary in the two-piece region
  is exact.
- The highlighted square and the outlines are computed from the functions themselves at their x, not interpolated.
- It refuses to draw anything that is not safe: pieces that do not join, boundaries that jump or are out of order,
  undefined values, a region with no width or too large, or no slice inside the region. When it refuses, no button
  is shown.

**`assets/unit6-3d/viewer.mjs`** draws the scene with three.js.
- three.js 0.169.0 (MIT) is vendored in `assets/unit6-3d/vendor/three/`, with its integrity hash recorded in
  `assets/unit6-3d/dependency-lock.json`. The only change is that OrbitControls imports the local three.js file.
- It loads (about 0.7 MB) only when a student opens a model.
- It draws only when the view changes; there is no animation loop.
- Hiding the model or moving to a new question releases its WebGL resources.
- If WebGL is unavailable, a short message says so and points to the 2D graph.

**`assets/unit6-3d/cross-sections-3d.mjs`** (loaded only on the non-calculator Cross Sections page) watches
`#question`. Whenever the practice driver writes a new question there, it:
1. releases the previous model, so stale geometry is never shown;
2. adds a closed toggle, only if the new problem is supported.

**Wiring and version**
- `tools/build-unit6-pages.py` adds the module to that one page.
- `assets/unit6.css` styles the toggle and the model.
- The version is 13.0.A in the app, service worker, analytics and Unit 6 page markers.
