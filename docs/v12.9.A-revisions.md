## A. Curriculum implementation summary

**Answer entry (every Unit 6 page).** Answers are typed with the existing math keypad and editor (cursor, Enter,
focus, fractions, exponents and mobile behavior unchanged). Kinds of answers:
- exact values (fractions, radicals, multiples of π, e, ln 2, ln 3);
- calculator values correct to three decimal places (the Unit 5 rounding rules);
- lists of x- or y-values;
- open intervals;
- expressions in x or y, such as a radius R(x) or a cross-sectional area A(y);
- integral setups;
- multiple choice only where a choice is the skill: which slices give one integral, and the reason for an
  inflection point or extremum.

**Integral setups.** A setup is typed as one to three integrals: [number] ∫ from [a] to [b] of [integrand] dx | dy.
- *How it is graded.* Any correct setup is accepted (washers or shells, dx or dy, π inside or outside, split or not).
  The setup is graded by its value. Where a question names the method, the variable must match it.
- *Notes.* A missing π or 2π, a reversed sign, (R − r)² and a mismatched method each get a specific note.
- *Calculator problems.* Limits typed as decimals with three or more places are accepted. The allowance is what the
  rounding can change: |integrand at the limit| × half a unit in the last place. Fewer places get a note to keep
  more digits.
- *Constant integrands.* A bare number is not accepted as an integrand (this stops typing the answer), except on an
  interval where the model integrand is itself constant.
- *Generation guard.* A problem whose common wrong setup has the same value is never generated: a width or radius not
  squared, (R − r)², or a shell without its radius.

**Free-response problems (in the reviews)**

*Area and volume, non-calculator:* one region, three or four parts. The area comes first (for x = y² with x = y + k,
a single dy integral). The remaining parts are cross sections, washers about a horizontal line, shells or washers
about a vertical line, and the value of one volume. Both setups and exact values appear.

*f(x) = k/(1 + x²) with y = c (2025–26 Test A and B, question 1):*
- the area, with arctangent; the curve meets the line at x = ±1, ±√3 or ±1/√3;
- a setup for the solid about a horizontal line above or below R;
- a cross-section setup (challenging tier);
- the rate of change of h(x), the vertical distance from (x, f(x)) to the line, inside or outside the interval.

*Area and volume, calculator* (the tests' first calculator question and its regions):
- the intersections;
- the area;
- cross sections perpendicular to x or y;
- volumes about horizontal and vertical lines, with a setup part at the challenging tier.

*Graph free response* (2025–26 Test A and B, questions 2 and 3):
- *The graph of f.* Line segments with a semicircle or quarter circles on grid points. Arcs touch the axis only at
  their ends or at the semicircle's lowest or highest point. Segments cross at grid points.
- *Format 1: f = g′ with g(c) given.* Parts: g at a point (often an endpoint); the points of inflection with their
  reason; the absolute maximum or minimum (Candidates Test); a limit that needs L'Hôpital's Rule (as on the test) or
  g″ at a point.
- *Format 2: g(x) = ∫_c^x f(t) dt.* Parts: g at points left and right of c (reversed integrals); the points of
  inflection or local extrema with their reason; intervals where g increases; the absolute maximum or minimum.
- *Values.* Every value is exact (rationals and multiples of π).
- *Reasons.* The reason is chosen from four statements, and each explanation ends with a complete written
  justification to compare against. **Written reasoning is not graded**: the site checks the values, the lists and
  the chosen reason, not the student's own sentences.

**Review mix**

- **Exact answers.**
  - Every exact area and volume equals its numeric integral.
  - Area with dx equals area with dy for every region.
  - Washers equal shells about six lines for every region.
- **Course results.**
  - The assignment keys are reproduced exactly: 89π/30, 31π/30, 11π/30, 23π/10, 49π/30 and 6π + 11π²/4.
  - Test A's 8/(1 + x²) with y = 4 gives 4π − 8.
  - Both 2025–26 graph questions are rebuilt piece by piece. Test A: g(8) = 16 − 2π, inflection points −4, 0, 2,
    4, 6, absolute minimum 0. Test B: g(−6) = 1, g(3) = 1 + 3π/4, inflection points −4, −2, 3, local maxima −5 and 1,
    local minimum −3.
- **Graph questions.** Every value of g is checked against numeric integration. Inflection points and extrema are
  checked against the direction and sign of f found numerically. Absolute extrema are checked against dense
  sampling.
- **Typed answers.** Every integral setup (244), typed as a student would, is graded correct; so is every exact
  value, list and interval. Typical wrong setups are graded wrong: no π, reversed limits, not squared, the other
  variable.
- **In real browsers** (desktop and phone): every Unit 6 page and Practice Type, 312 problems and 575 parts entered
  through the keypad and keyboard, all graded correct, with no console errors, raw TeX, scroll boxes or overflow.
- **Site-wide QA:** the same eight scripts fail on `main` and here, with the same errors. `qa:unit6` and the deep
  audit (63 topic pages) pass.

## E. Teacher testing checklist
1. Unit 6 topic pages: Area, Cross Sections, Solids and the Review show a Non-Calculator button and a Calculator button
   (with the calculator icon). Area in Terms of y and Piecewise keep one Practice button.
2. The old URLs `…/area-below-and-between-curves/practice/`, `…/volume-by-cross-sections/practice/`,
   `…/solids-of-revolution/practice/` and `…/comprehensive-review/assignment-practice/` open the topic page.
3. On each page the Practice Type menu has the choices listed in A. Each choice gives only those problems:
   Perpendicular to the y-axis gives only dy cross sections, and Cylindrical Shells only shells.
4. Calculator pages show "Calculator active" on every problem. Non-calculator pages and the Non-Calculator Review
   never do.
5. Integral setups:
   - type 1, 2 or 3 integrals with "+ Add another integral" and choose dx or dy;
   - try a correct washer setup and a correct shell setup for the same solid (both accepted unless the question
     names the method);
   - try dropping π, or writing (R − r)² (graded wrong, with a note);
   - on a calculator problem, type limits to three decimals (accepted).
6. Radii and A(x): type R(x) and r(x) on a washer problem and A(x) on a cross-section problem; the explanations
   measure radii from the axis.
7. Graph Free Response (Non-Calculator Review): the graph matches its description. Check g at an endpoint, the
   inflection points, the reason, the absolute extremum and the limit against the worked explanation and its
   written justification.
8. Area and Volume Free Response: the cap-line problem (k/(1 + x²) with y = c), a region problem with cross sections
   and rotations, and a calculator problem with intersections and volumes.
9. Explanations: each starts by finding the bounds, sets up with the correct slices, and evaluates step by step
   (antiderivative, values at the limits).
10. Phone: the integral control wraps (dx and dy below), the figures fit, the keypad works.
11. Unit 5 Growth and Decay: the button and page say Calculator Practice, the button has the calculator icon, and the
    problems are unchanged.

## Limitations (stated plainly)
- **Written explanations are not graded.** "Explain your reasoning" is a choice among reasons, followed by a model
  written justification.
- **Integral setups are graded by value.** An unusual but correct setup is accepted. A wrong setup that happens to
  have the same value would also be accepted, so problems where a common wrong setup ties are not generated.
- **Piecewise.** The Integrals on Piecewise Defined Functions page still uses its old generator, unchanged. Its trig
  variant can show a choice "(c − 1) + 1" equal to the correct one. The review skips such problems; the standalone
  page still shows them.
- **Calculator intersections** are given in the explanation to four decimals. Students should store the values.
