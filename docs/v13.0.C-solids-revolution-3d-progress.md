## 2. Geometry and volumes (Node): 53,903 checks, 0 failures

For every rotation (the 644 fixtures and the 1,560 generated rotations):

- **The mesh.** It is:
  - closed: its divergence-theorem volume is unchanged when it is moved;
  - outward: its volume is positive;
  - equal to the exact volume of the revolved boundary polygon (Pappus) times 128 sin(2π/128)/(2π) = 0.999598, to
    2·10⁻⁶ (float32 rounding).
- **Radii from the actual axis.** Every checked vertex of the solid lies on a circle about the actual axis (y = c, z = 0
  or x = c, z = 0). The circle's radius is the distance from that axis to a point of the region's boundary, at that
  point's position along the axis (to 2·10⁻⁶ relative). This holds for shifted axes too: the hole is exactly where the
  region stays away from the axis.
- **Both methods, computed independently.** The volume was integrated from the region's slices by washers,
  π∫(R² − r²), and by shells, 2π∫(radius)(height), whenever the region has both kinds of slices. The two agree to 10⁻⁷
  in 1,755 cases, and they equal the problem's own answer for that rotation (exact value, setup value or calculator
  value) to 10⁻⁷. For two-axis problems, each rotation was compared with its own answer.
- **Mesh vs integral.** The revolved polygon differs from the exact volume only through the sampling of the boundary
  (72 cosine-spaced points per piece). The largest differences by kind:

  | Kind | Largest difference | Case |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | disk | −0.079% | x = 2y² − y³ about the y-axis (steep where the curve meets the axis) |
  | shell | +0.063% | |
  | washer | −0.057% | |
  | two axes | +0.063% | |

  Doubling the sampling divides the difference by 3.99 or more (worst ratio 0.2501): second order. No tolerance was
  relaxed. The test allows 2·10⁻³ and requires that ratio to be at most 0.3.
- **The highlighted slice** is the explanation's own slice. It is the same region object (by identity of the boundary
  functions), the same position, and end points equal to the 2D picture's to the last digit.
  - **Disks and washers.** The outer radius R and inner radius r equal the engine's own R(t) and r(t) at that slice
    (to 10⁻⁹). For a disk, r = 0 and the drawn disk has no hole. The drawn slab spans r…R about the axis, centred at
    the slice.
  - **Shells.** The radius equals the engine's r(t) and the height its h(t). The drawn shell spans radius ± half its
    thickness and runs along the whole slice.
  - **Typed parts.** The 202 typed R, r, radius and height parts were compared: each graded answer function equals the
    model's distance at the slice.
- **The axis line** lies on the axis, and the region lies on one side of it.
- **Descriptor.** It is attached to all seven families, never enumerable, and never in the JSON.

## 3. Browser: Solids of Revolution (Chromium, real pages)

`tools/review-lab/unit6/solids3d-browser.mjs` ran at 1366×900 with a mouse and 390×844 with touch: **30 runs, 0
failures, 0 console errors.** Every family was covered, with and without a graph in the question. The axes were the
coordinate axes and shifted lines on every side, with disks, washers and both kinds of shells.

- **Placement and timing**, in every run:
  - **Graph in the question:** one closed toggle right after the graph. For two axes, Part A's toggle sits under Part
    A's prompt. Part B's appears under Part B's prompt only when Part B is shown, and it is about Part B's axis.
  - **No graph in the question** (non-calculator) **and every calculator question:** nothing at the start, no toggle,
    no model, and no three.js request. Each rotation's toggle appears right after the region's picture in the
    explanation of the part that first draws it, and only once that part is checked.
  - For two axes: Part A's model after Part A, and Part B's after Part B. All 8 two-axis runs gave two models with two
    different axes.
- **Geometry read back from the GPU buffers**, against independent formulas in the page:
  - the solid spans the region along the axis;
  - it reaches exactly the region's least and greatest distances from the axis;
  - the highlighted disk, washer or shell has R and r (or radius and height) equal to the slice's distances from the
    axis;
  - the pink rod lies on y = c or x = c with z = 0;
  - the labels are the axis equation and R, r or r, h;
  - the surface is smooth;
  - the canvas has drawn, yellow and pink pixels.
- **Answers.** Every part of every problem (57 parts) was answered with its own answer and graded correct, with models
  open. 38 models were opened and checked.
- **Controls** (checked on 10 models, desktop and phone):
  - mouse drag or one finger rotates; the wheel or a pinch zooms;
  - Reset view restores the view;
  - there were 0 redraws while idle;
  - hiding released every GPU geometry and texture;
  - five quick clicks left at most one canvas.
- **Next question.** With one or two models open, the next question released them all (0 left) and showed no toggle
  until its own picture appeared.
- **Memory.** 10 open/close cycles per viewport released all memory each time.
- **Layout.** No page was wider than the screen.
- **No WebGL.** The message read "This browser cannot show the 3D model (WebGL is not available). The 2D graph still
  shows the region and the axis."; the graph stayed, and there were 0 console errors.
- **Screenshots, reviewed visually.** Every family was checked at 1366, and several at 390. For example:
  - about x = 2 the triangle's solid is a cylinder with a conical hole;
  - about x = 10 the region under 3√x makes a dome with a hole of radius 1;

  - the shells are cylinders around the pink axis.

## 6. Limitations

- **Testing.** The model is a Beta visual aid. There was no physical-device or assistive-technology testing. Touch was
  tested with real touch events in Chromium's device emulation. Safari and Firefox were not tested.
- **When a model appears.** A model appears only where the region's 2D picture for that rotation is shown. For the
  non-calculator questions that do not show a graph (about half, chosen by the generator), it therefore appears with
  the first explanation, not in the question. This is a deliberate choice: the question deliberately leaves the region
  for the student to sketch, and calculator regions for the calculator.
- **Accuracy.**
  - The solid samples the boundary at 72 cosine-spaced points per piece. Its volume is within 0.079% of the exact
    value, largest where a curve meets the axis steeply (x = 2y² − y³).
  - Around the axis it has 128 segments, 0.040% below the true circle.
- **Thickness.** The highlighted disk, washer or shell is given a small thickness as a visual aid; the radii and the
  height are exact.
- **The view.** It opens like the 2D graph (x right, y up), so for some solids the axis runs toward the viewer at
  first. The view can be turned freely, and Reset view restores it.
- **The Comprehensive Review pages** do not show the models. They were left unchanged.
