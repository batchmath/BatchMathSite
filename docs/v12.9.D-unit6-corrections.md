## 1. Why an integral is split

**Reported case.** Volume by Cross Sections, Non-Calculator Practice:
- the region bounded by y = 2√x, y = x − 8 and y = 0;
- rectangles with height twice the base, perpendicular to the x-axis.

The volume needs two integrals, on [0, 8] and [8, 16]. The explanation showed the two slice lengths but not why the
integral splits at x = 8.

**Now.** Every Unit 6 explanation of a setup with several integrals says, before the setup:

1. **Where** the split is. The breakpoint is located by the equation of the two boundaries that meet there, for
   example "x − 8 = 0 when x = 8". Calculator breakpoints appear as "≈" values, marked "calculator". When the equation
   would only repeat the breakpoint, the text just says that x = y meets x = 2 at y = 2.
2. **What** changes:
   - the upper or lower boundary, or the left or right boundary;
   - two curves changing places;
   - a formula of a piecewise function;
   - the shape of a graph.

   The boundary that does not change is named too.
3. **Why** it matters. The quantity the method uses has a different formula on each side, so one integrand cannot
   be used on the whole interval. That quantity is:
   - the slice length s for cross sections;
   - the height h(x) or length ℓ(y) for area;
   - the outer or inner radius for washers, and disks becoming washers;
   - the shell height for shells.
4. **How** it is split. The changing quantity is shown piecewise, then one integral per piece.

For the reported case the explanation reads:

> **Split the integral at x = 8.** At x = 8, y = x − 8 meets the x-axis, y = 0: x − 8 = 0 when x = 8. Before x = 8 the
> lower boundary of the region is the x-axis, y = 0; after it, the lower boundary is y = x − 8. The upper boundary,
> y = 2√x, is the same on both sides. Because the lower boundary changes, the slice length s(x) has a different formula
> on each side of x = 8, so one integrand cannot be used on the whole interval: the integral must be split at x = 8,
> with one integral for each piece.
> s(x) = { 2√x, 0 ≤ x ≤ 8; 2√x − (x − 8), 8 ≤ x ≤ 16 }

It then reads: "On each piece, A(x) = 2(s(x))², with the slice length s(x) of that piece", followed by
V = 2∫₀⁸(2√x)² dx + 2∫₈¹⁶(2√x − (x − 8))² dx.

**Where.** One region-aware routine in `assets/unit6-regions.js` produces these steps: `splitStep`, with `breaks`,
`changeSummary` and `bname`. It is used by `areaWork`, `sectionWork`, `washerWork` and `shellWork`, so it covers:
- Area Below and Between Curves (both calculator kinds, and Choosing dx or dy);
- Finding Area in Terms of y;
- Volume by Cross Sections;
- Solids of Revolution;
- the Area & Volume multipart problems;
- both comprehensive reviews.

The families with their own explanations were rewritten in the same terms:

| Family | What the explanation now says |
| --- | --- |
| Area with sign changes (`axis_sign`, `y_axis_cross`) | The area counts every region as positive, while ∫f is negative below the axis. The text says honestly that the area is ∫\|f\|, a single integral, which still has to be split to evaluate it. |
| Curves that change places (`between_switch`) | The crossing is derived, then which curve is on top on each side. |
| The dx/dy comparison | It names the boundary that changes, and where. |
| "Another way" setups | They say why they need several integrals. |
| Integrals on piecewise functions | Where each formula changes, and why one integrand cannot cross that point. |
| Graph Analysis & Accumulation | The split into known shapes (triangles, trapezoids, arcs) is described as a way to find the signed area. It is still one integral of f, so no necessity is claimed. |

**Optional splits are not presented as necessary.**
- Where slicing the other way gives one integral, area explanations say so. This is checked to be true: the other
  direction has one piece with the same value.
- No split is called necessary unless the formula really changes. If the formulas on both sides were the same, the
  text would say the integrals could be combined. No generated problem currently has this case.

**Phones.** A row of a piecewise brace cannot wrap. A brace is used only when its estimated width fits a 390px
phone. The threshold was calibrated in the browser: every brace estimated up to 20.97 em fit, and every one from
21.29 em overflowed. Wider expressions get one line per piece. `assets/unit6.css` sets braces at 86% below 420px.

**Display fix.** A shell setup with a constant height now reads 2π∫ y dy instead of 2π∫ y1 dy. This changes the
display only.

**No mathematics changed.**
- 2,060 of 2,070 problems compared seed by seed with v12.9.C have identical question text, prompts, answers and
  grading data.
- The other 10 differ only by the shell display fix.

## 2. Semicircles: the diameter lies across the region

**Reported case.** y = −x² + 4 and y = 0, with semicircular cross sections perpendicular to the y-axis, described as
"semicircles whose radius lies across the region". The diagram drew the slice as a radius.

**Convention, now everywhere.** The slice across the region is the semicircle's diameter:
- d = s;
- r = s/2;
- A = πs²/8.

For the reported case:
- s(y) = 2√(4 − y);
- A(y) = (π/8)(2√(4 − y))²;
- V = 4π. The old radius-across value was 16π.

**Quarter circles are unchanged.** The slice is a radius, r = s, and A = πs²/4.

**What changed**
- **Shapes.** `semicircle_r` (A = (π/2)s²) is removed from `assets/unit6-core.js`: the shape, its picture and its
  name. No problem regenerates from a stored shape name, so no legacy path was needed.
- **Shape pool.** In `assets/unit6-volume.js`, `semicircle_r` is replaced in place by `semicircle_d`. Semicircles
  keep their 20% share, and every other shape keeps its share and its seeds.
- **Other engines.** The multipart engine (`assets/unit6-frq.js`) and both reviews draw from the same pool.
- **Mathematics.** Question wording, areas, volumes, answer models and grading all follow from the one shape. The old
  answers (16π, a π/2 setup) are refused.
- **Explanation.** It connects the slice, diameter, radius and area: "The slice across the region is the diameter
  of the semicircle: d = s. So its radius is r = s/2, and its area is ½πr² = ½π(s/2)² = (π/8)s²." Quarter circles
  say the slice is a radius.
- **Picture.**
  - The semicircle is highlighted across its whole diameter, labelled d = s, with its centre and a radius labelled
    s/2.
  - The base text adds: "For a semicircle the whole slice is its diameter, so its radius is s/2."
  - Squares, rectangles, triangles and quarter circles are unchanged.
  - `qa:unit6` checks every picture against its shape's definition.

**Problems whose answers changed (compared with v12.9.C).** In the seed-by-seed comparison, the only answer changes
are:
- the problems that used to draw the radius-across semicircle, each now with the diameter (16 cross-section and 17
  multipart problems in the compared samples);
- the shell display fix.
