# v13.0.C: interactive 3D Solids of Revolution (Beta)

This experiment extends the Version 13 3D models from Volume by Cross Sections to every problem of the Solids of
Revolution engine. It is on branch `claude/v13.0c-solids-revolution-3d`, created from the completed v13.0.B branch at
`8480910`, which goes back through v13.0.A to `main` at `9dbb4f8` (v12.9.G). It is not production: `main` is unchanged
and nothing is deployed. All of v13.0.B's Volume by Cross Sections models are kept unchanged: the same 19 reference
questions render to identical pixels.

## What is supported

Every family of `assets/unit6-volume.js`'s Solids of Revolution engine, every tier, both pages:

| Family | Page | Rotations | Methods in the explanation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `disk_basic` | Non-Calculator | 1 | disks |
| `washer` | Non-Calculator | 1 | washers, or disks when the region touches the axis |
| `shells` | Non-Calculator | 1 | shells about vertical lines (dx) and horizontal lines (dy) |
| `two_axes` | Non-Calculator | 2 | washers about the first axis, shells about the second |
| `trig_solid` | Non-Calculator | 1 | washers, y = A + sin x about y = −c or y = d |
| `solid_calc` | Calculator | 1 | disks, washers or shells, including the v10.6 calculator solids |
| `calc_two_axes` | Calculator | 2 | each rotation with its own method |

Axes:
- the x-axis and the y-axis (when the region lies on one side);
- horizontal lines y = c above or below the region;
- vertical lines x = c to its left or right;
- lines along its boundary.

Regions:
- every non-calculator region the engine uses: pairs of power curves, √x and powers with lines, caps, half caps,
  triangles, the reciprocal, x = ay² − y³, the wedge, the semicircle;
- the trigonometric region;
- the calculator regions: a·ln(x+1) with a line, 1 + ln x with x² − d, e^(kx) with the axes, k·cos x with a·x²;
- the v10.6 regions under a curve.

The generator's own numbers are used throughout, with calculator intersections at full precision. No valid case is
unsupported.

As a safety net, the geometry module refuses a rotation it cannot draw safely, with its exact reason, and the 2D
pictures stay. The reasons are:
- the axis cuts the region;
- unknown axis;
- undefined or out-of-order boundaries;
- no area;
- too large;
- no slice;
- unknown method;
- a shell on the axis.

None of the generated problems in the tests is refused.

## What a student sees

**The button.** It is the same as before: **Show 3D Model (Beta)** / **Hide 3D Model**, closed at first. The model
has the same stage, colours, grid, axes, Reset view, help line, mouse and touch controls, lazy loading and release.

**When a model appears.** A model appears exactly where the region's 2D picture for that rotation is, never earlier:
- **A non-calculator question that shows the region and its axis:** right after that graph.
- **The same with two rotations (two_axes):** each rotation's model sits with its own part, under that part's prompt,
  once the part is reached. Part A's model is about Part A's axis, and Part B's about Part B's.
- **A question that does not show the region**, which covers every calculator question and the non-calculator
  questions without a graph: the model appears right after the region's picture in the explanation of the part that
  first draws that rotation, once that part is checked.
  - Students graph calculator regions on their own calculators. Nothing of the model is in the page before then, so
    no region, setup or answer is revealed early.
  - For two rotations, Part A's model comes with Part A's explanation and Part B's with Part B's.

**The model**, at one scale on every axis:
- **The region** is gold, in the xy-plane, with its outline.
- **The axis of rotation** is a distinct pink rod along y = c or x = c (z = 0), labelled with its equation and named
  in the legend.
- **The solid** is translucent blue: the region turned through a full revolution. It is smooth around the axis, and it
  has a hole wherever the region stays away from the axis.
- **Outlines.** The circles traced by the region's corners are drawn, with light outlines of the region turned to
  eight positions.
- **The representative slice** is yellow, at the explanation's own slice, given a small thickness as a visual aid:
  - a **disk**, with radius R;
  - a **washer**, with R toward the region (as in the 2D picture) and r on the other side of the axis;
  - a **cylindrical shell**, with radius r from the axis to the slice and height h along the slice.
- **The caption** names the rotation ("R revolved about the line y = −2"; with the part for two rotations).
- **The view.** It opens like the 2D graph (x to the right, y up the screen), seen a little from the right and above,
  so the region keeps its familiar orientation and the solid comes out of the page around the axis. It can be turned
  freely.
- **Nothing is given away.** No volume, integral or numerical value is shown.

## How it is built

**Data from the generator.** `describeRevolution(R, rotations, graph)` in `assets/unit6-volume.js` builds a frozen
descriptor, attached as the non-enumerable `u6Revolution` by all seven families. It holds:
- the region's slices as the generator's own boundary functions (perpendicular to the x-axis, the y-axis or both);
- the equations;
- for each rotation:
  - its axis;
  - the explanation's method;
  - the explanation's representative slice (from `sliceOf`, which the explanation's picture now also uses, with
    identical output);
  - the part that first asks about it, and the part whose explanation first draws it;
- whether the question shows the graph.

Nothing random is drawn, and questions, answers, explanations and stored data are unchanged.

**`assets/unit6-3d/revolution-geometry.mjs` (pure, tested in Node)**
- **Boundary.** The region's boundary is a closed counterclockwise polygon, sampled densely from the boundary functions
  (cosine spacing, every breakpoint a sample).
- **Revolution.** That polygon is turned about the actual axis line: (x, c + (y − c) cos t, (y − c) sin t) for y = c,
  and (c + (x − c) cos t, y, (x − c) sin t) for x = c. This gives a closed, outward, indexed surface: 128 segments
  around the axis, normals turned with the points, split at the boundary's corners.
- **Method-independent.** This is the solid itself. It is the same whichever method computes the volume, and its radii
  are measured from the real axis, wherever it is.
- **Representative slice.** It is the explanation's slice, thickened and turned about the same axis.

**Shared code (refactored without changing the cross-section models)**
- `assets/unit6-3d/scene-kit.mjs`: renderer, stage, grid, axes, labels, camera fit, controls, render on change, resize
  and release, taken from the v13.0.B viewer.
- `assets/unit6-3d/model-toggle.mjs`: the Show/Hide control with lazy loading and release.
- `assets/unit6-3d/viewer.mjs` (cross sections) and `cross-sections-3d.mjs` now use them. Their output is
  pixel-identical to v13.0.B (`tools/review-lab/unit6/cs3d-pixels.mjs`).
- `assets/unit6-3d/revolution-viewer.mjs` draws the solid of revolution.
- `assets/unit6-3d/solids-3d.mjs` places the models on the two Solids of Revolution practice pages.

**Wiring and version**
- `tools/build-unit6-pages.py` adds `solids-3d.mjs` to both Solids of Revolution practice pages, before the MathLive
  tags.
- The version is 13.0.C in the app, service worker, analytics and Unit 6 page markers.

## Tests

See `docs/v13.0.C-solids-revolution-3d-progress.md` for the exact results. The tests are:
- `tools/review-lab/unit6/qa/revolution3d.mjs`: geometry, volumes by both methods, radii and heights against the
  engine's own terms, the coverage table and refusals, in Node;

  the WebGL-off case;
- `tools/review-lab/unit6/cs3d-pixels.mjs`: the Volume by Cross Sections models compared pixel by pixel with v13.0.B;
- `tools/review-lab/unit6/cs3d-browser.mjs`: the full v13.0.B cross-section browser test;
- `tools/review-lab/unit6/qa/regress-trees.mjs`: a seed-by-seed comparison of every Unit 6 engine and the review.
