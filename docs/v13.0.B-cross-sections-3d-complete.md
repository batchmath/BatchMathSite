# v13.0.B: complete interactive 3D Volume by Cross Sections (Beta)

This experiment extends the v13.0.A prototype to every problem of the Volume by Cross Sections engine. It is on branch
`claude/v13.0b-cross-sections-3d-complete`, created from the v13.0.A branch at `3993639`, which was created from
`main` at `9dbb4f8` (v12.9.G). It is not production: `main` is unchanged and nothing is deployed.

## What is supported

Every problem the engine (`assets/unit6-volume.js`) generates is supported:

| | Covered |
| --- | --- |
| Shapes (8) | square; rectangle of height 2s; rectangle of height 3s; semicircle with the slice as its diameter; quarter circle with the slice as its radius; equilateral triangle; isosceles right triangle with a leg on the slice; isosceles right triangle with the hypotenuse on the slice |
| Directions | slices perpendicular to the x-axis and perpendicular to the y-axis |
| Families and tiers | `cs_basic` (foundational, intermediate), `cs_setup` (intermediate, challenging), `cs_calc` (intermediate, challenging) |
| Pages | Non-Calculator Practice and Calculator Practice |
| Regions | caps, half caps, semicircles, disks, triangles, pairs of power curves, √x and powers with a line, y = H with xᵖ, y = eˣ with y = 1, the two-piece regions (√x with a line and the x-axis; the wedge between two lines and x = b), and the calculator regions (a·ln(x+1) with a line, 1 + ln x with x² − d, e^(kx) with the axes, k·cos x with a·x²), with one or several pieces |

No problem type is unsupported. As a safety net, the geometry module refuses any descriptor it cannot draw safely, and
records the exact reason. Such a problem shows no button and keeps its 2D graph. The refusal reasons are:
- pieces that do not join;
- boundaries that jump;
- undefined or out-of-order boundaries;
- no width;
- a region that is too large;
- a slice outside the region;
- an unknown shape or direction;
- an area factor that is not the drawn shape's.

None of the generated problems in the tests is refused.

## What a student sees

- **The button.** It is the same as v13.0.A: **Show 3D Model (Beta)**, then **Hide 3D Model** when open. The model has
  the same stage, colours, axes, grid, Reset view, help line, mouse and touch controls, and lazy loading.
- **Non-calculator questions.** The button sits right after the 2D Show Graph control, as in v13.0.A.
- **Calculator questions.** Students graph the region on their own calculators, and the page has always shown it only
  in Part A's explanation, after Part A is checked. The 3D button follows the same rule:
  - nothing of the model is in the page before Part A is checked;
  - once Part A is checked (right or wrong), the button appears right after the region's first picture in Part A's
    explanation;
  - the model stays there while the student works on Part B, and goes with the next question.
- **The solid.** The base region is gold and the solid translucent blue. Faces swept by arcs (semicircles, quarter
  circles) are smooth-shaded in a lighter blue, so curved surfaces are distinguishable from flat ones; faces swept by
  straight sides are flat-shaded. The solid's edges are drawn, and nine lighter cross-section outlines along it show how
  the sections stack.
- **The highlighted cross section** (yellow) stands on the slice that the 2D picture highlights, with the labels of the
  2D cross-section picture:

  | Shape | Labels |
  | --- | --- |
  | square | s, s |
  | rectangle | s, 2s or 3s |
  | semicircle | d = s, and r = s/2 on a drawn radius |
  | quarter circle | r = s on the slice, s on the other radius |
  | equilateral triangle | s on each side |
  | isosceles right triangle, leg | s, s and a right-angle mark |
  | isosceles right triangle, hypotenuse | s and a right-angle mark at the apex |

- **The legend** names the shape, for example "solid made of semicircular cross sections" and "one cross section: a
  semicircle with diameter d = s (radius s/2)".
- **Nothing is given away.** No volume, integral, area formula or numerical value is shown.

## How it is built

**Data from the generator.** `describeSolid(R, v, shape)` in `assets/unit6-volume.js` builds a frozen descriptor. It is
attached as the non-enumerable `u6Solid` by `famCsBasic`, `famCsSetup` and now `famCsCalc`. It holds:
- each piece's interval;
- its lo and hi boundary as the generator's own functions: bot(x)/top(x) for slices on x, left(y)/right(y) for slices
  on y;
- the shape and multiplier;
- the shape's area factor k (A = k s²), from the same `shape.k` the answer uses;
- the slice the 2D picture uses (`sliceAt`);
- the equations and the region kind.

Calculator regions keep their intersections at full precision (the generator's `bn(p)` values). Nothing random is
drawn, and the problem's JSON is unchanged.

**`assets/unit6-3d/solid-geometry.mjs` (pure, tested in Node)**

- **Profiles.** Each shape is a profile in local coordinates (u along the slice from its lo end, w up), starting with
  the slice itself:

  | Shape | Profile |
  | --- | --- |
  | square | s × s |
  | rectangle | s × m·s |
  | semicircle | centre at the slice's midpoint, radius s/2, so the whole slice is the diameter |
  | quarter circle | centre at the lo end, radius s, the other radius vertical |
  | equilateral triangle | apex at (s/2, √3/2·s) |
  | isosceles right triangle, leg | right angle at the lo end, legs s and s |
  | isosceles right triangle, hypotenuse | apex at (s/2, s/2), legs s/√2 |

  The quarter circle and the leg triangle put their right angle at the lo end, as the 2D pictures do.
- **Orientation.** The profile is swept along the slicing variable t, in its own orientation:
  - perpendicular to the x-axis: (x, y, z) = (t, lo(t) + u, w);
  - perpendicular to the y-axis: (x, y, z) = (lo(t) + u, t, w).

  Neither is a rotated version of the other. The same scale is used on every axis.
- **Mesh.** The result is a closed, outward-oriented mesh: the base, flat faces, curved faces with radial normals, and
  end sections.
- **Sampling.** Samples use cosine spacing on each piece, and every breakpoint is a sample.
- **Exact sections.** The highlighted section and the outlines are computed from the boundary functions at their t.
- **Arcs.** Arc vertices lie exactly on the circle: 64 segments for the semicircle and 32 for the quarter circle. The
  drawn arc polygon's area is the circle's area times 0.999598 (the chord factor), which the tests account for
  explicitly.

**`assets/unit6-3d/viewer.mjs`** draws the scene with the vendored three.js 0.169.0, which is unchanged from v13.0.A.
- It uses separate materials for flat and curved faces, shape labels as text sprites, and right-angle marks.
- The default view looks at the sections partly face on. For slices on y the camera turns with the sections.
- It draws only when the view changes.
- The WebGL2 context is requested directly, so a browser without WebGL gets the short message without a console error.
- Hiding the model or changing the question releases every GPU geometry and texture. The amounts released are recorded
  for the tests.

**`assets/unit6-3d/cross-sections-3d.mjs`** is loaded on both Cross Sections practice pages. It watches `#question`
(a new question) and `#choices` (Part A's explanation on calculator problems). When the question changes, it:
1. releases the previous model, so stale geometry is never shown;
2. adds a closed toggle at the right place for the new problem.

**Wiring and version**
- `tools/build-unit6-pages.py` adds the module to both Cross Sections practice pages, before the MathLive tags.
- The version is 13.0.B in the app, service worker, analytics and Unit 6 page markers.
