Base: the v12.9.A prerelease (`dccdd15`), through the display checkpoint branch
`claude/v12.9b-unit6-display-checkpoint` (`0d4dfba`, checkpoints 1–8). Branch: `claude/v12.9b-unit6-teacher-corrections`.
`main` (the deployed v12.8.Y, `26ec4f1`), the v12.9.A branch and prerelease, and the checkpoint branch are unchanged.
Nothing is merged or deployed. Commits and checks: `docs/v12.9.B-progress.md`. Entry formats for the separate MathLive

All problems are original BatchMath problems generated from parameters. None reproduces a released College Board

The AP trademark notice is unchanged on every page.

## A. What changed

### Kept from the display checkpoints (1–8)
1. No redundant outer parentheses (x = 1 − y², not x = (1 − y²)).
2. No written coefficients of 1 (ln(x + 1), x, x²).
3. No "Use your calculator" on calculator problems (the badge and the page already say so).
4. One rounding instruction: keep intersections at full precision, round only the final answer.
5. Signed-area explanations show the combining arithmetic (A = 63/8 − (−125/24) + 19/6 = 65/4).
6. Compact Area Below and Between Curves graphs (at most 340 px wide and about 265 px tall).
7. Area in terms of y: no supplied bounds the curves already define; the explanation finds them.
8. Calculator cross sections: no graph in the question; the graph opens Part A's explanation only.

The checks for 1–3 (`display-scan.mjs`) and for 6 (tick size and figure caps) were rerun on the final code. The
v12.9.B changes below build on them (checkpoint 8's explanation graph now also shows a slice and the cross section).

### Area Below and Between Curves (calculator and non-calculator)
- **Direction tasks** (`area_directions`, `calc_directions`): with respect to x only, y only, either (the
  explanation then shows the other method too, "Another way."), or both.
- **Setup only, setup and exact area, setup and calculator value.**
- **Intersections** are asked for only when no graph shows them (enclosed regions, intermediate tier).
- **Graphs**: the compact graph appears in the question about half the time in the families that show one. Overall
  that is 19.9% of non-calculator questions and 55.7% of calculator questions; the calculator intersection problems
  always show it, as in v12.9.A. When the question shows none, the explanation does (signed areas with the pieces
  above and below the axis, switching curves, enclosed regions).
- **Choosing dx or dy**: a new Practice Type on the non-calculator page. Students type a single integral in the better
  direction, then find the area. Challenging problems also ask for the setup in the other direction. There is no
  multiple choice. The better direction is x in 49% of problems and y in 51%.
- The reciprocal-and-line region accepts its dy setup (the region has the same shape in y) and says so; it no longer
  says "Any correct setup is accepted".

### Finding Area in Terms of y
- A graph in every explanation.
- Mixed equation forms (`y_mixed`, 30% of the page): a boundary given as y = f(x) is rewritten as x = g(y) in the
  explanation (lines, roots, line pairs, powers).
- The former "choose dx or dy" family moved to the Choosing dx or dy Practice Type (above).

### Integrals on Piecewise Defined Functions
- Typed exact answers (fractions, integers, multiples of π, ln 2 and their sums) with the exact-value keypad, in place
  of multiple choice. Practice page and the Non-Calculator Review use the new engine (`assets/unit6-piecewise.js`).
- The same six families and parameter ranges. All 369 parameter sets that both versions produced give identical
  values, and 600 seeded problems match the closed forms.
- The explanation splits the integral at each point where the formula changes, evaluates each branch on its own
  interval (antiderivative and value), and adds.
- Using one formula over the whole interval gets a note (two-piece families).

### Volume by Cross Sections
- **Non-calculator**: every question has a **Show Graph / Hide Graph** control, closed at first.
- **Explanations** (both pages): Part A's explanation shows the base with one slice highlighted, perpendicular to the
  x-axis or the y-axis as the problem says. Next to it is the cross section standing on that slice, drawn flat: a square,
  a rectangle 2s or 3s high, a semicircle on a diameter or on a radius, a quarter circle, an equilateral triangle, or an
  isosceles right triangle on a leg or on the hypotenuse, with its area formula.
- **Directions**: 50/50 on the non-calculator page. The calculator page now chooses the direction first, so it is
  50/50 there too (it was 71/29 toward x).
- **Calculator**: checkpoint 8 is kept (no graph until Part A is graded, only in Part A's explanation).

### Solids of Revolution
- **Non-calculator**: the question shows the graph in about 50% of problems, in every family. Graphs use one scale for
  both axes, and the axis of rotation is always inside the frame (teacher PDF 9). The two-axis problems draw both axes.
- **Explanations**: the first explanation of each rotation shows the region, the axis and one slice. For a disk it
  shows R from the axis; for a washer, R and r from the axis; for a shell, the radius r and the height h. Next to it is
  the disk or washer seen face on, or the shell (a shell about a horizontal axis is drawn on its side). The existing
  steps explain the method and the variable ("the axis is horizontal, so slice perpendicular to it…").
- **Calculator**: no graph in the question and no Show Graph button. Part A's explanation shows the region, the axis
  and the slice. The v10.6 formula problems build their region for this.
- **Calculator axes** by kind, each chosen about equally where the region allows it: the x-axis, the y-axis, lines
  above, below, left and right of the region, and a line on the region's boundary.
- **Connected multipart problems** (`calc_two_axes`, 31% of the calculator page): the same region revolved about two
  different lines (a horizontal and a vertical one when possible), a volume for each. Challenging problems ask for the
  second setup.
- Setups accept washers or shells when both apply to the region and axis, and only then say so.
- All 2,528 sampled volume answers agree with Pappus' theorem (2π × centroid distance × area).

### Integral grading (all Unit 6 setups)
v12.9.A graded a typed setup by its value. v12.9.B checks the setup:
- **Accepted**: the question's setup, the alternatives the problem lists (the other variable or method where the
  question allows it), and twice the integral over half of a symmetric region. Equivalent forms of these are also
  accepted: expanded or factored, the constant inside or outside, split intervals, reversed limits with the opposite
  sign.
- **Refused**:
  - a setup that only has the right value;
  - a method or variable the question excludes (it gets the method note);
  - common mistakes, each with its note: (R − r)², a missing π or 2π, unsquared radii or widths, a shell without its
    radius.
- **Wording**: no prompt or help says "Any correct method/setup is accepted". The free-response setups that do not
  name a method say "Washers or shells: either method is accepted" only when the other method is an accepted model;
  otherwise they name the method the region needs.
- **Tests**: `integral-entry.mjs` and the new `checker-adversarial.mjs`. Section D gives the results.

### Renames (labels only)
- "Area and Volume Free Response" is now **"Area & Volume — Multi-Part Practice"**.
- "Graph Free Response" is now **"Graph Analysis & Accumulation — Multi-Part Practice"**.

These names appear in the review Practice Type menus, the review topic tags and the review page descriptions. The
values `frq_region` and `frq_graph`, the engines, the URLs and the math of these problems are unchanged.

### MathLive
Not part of v12.9.B. Codex runs the sitewide migration separately. Unit 6 typed entry still uses the BatchMath
keypad and works as before. `docs/v12.9.B-unit6-mathlive-handoff.md` lists the entry formats, grader contracts and
tests a MathLive adapter for Unit 6 must meet. The Unit 6 migration is not done until that adapter exists and passes
them.

### Deferred (documented, not implemented)
**Collapsing Part A's explanation.** In multipart problems, a graded part's explanation stays open above the next
part. Collapsing it once the student continues, with a control to reopen it, would change the shared multipart UI
(`assets/unit5-ui.js`) on every Unit 5 and Unit 6 page. It is left for a separate, sitewide change.

The approach would be:
1. Wrap each completed part's explanation in a closed `<details>` with a "Show explanation" summary.
2. Keep it open for the part just graded.
3. Close it when Continue is pressed.
4. Check the entry tool and the graph-after check against that behavior.

## D. Teacher testing checklist
1. **Area, non-calculator.** Open All Problems and Choosing dx or dy.
   - Setups ask for x only, y only, either, or both.
   - "Either" explanations show the other way too.
   - Intersections are asked only when no graph is shown.
   - Choosing dx or dy has no multiple choice.
2. **Area, calculator.** Calculator setups in x, in y, either and both. Values are rounded only at the end.
3. **Area in terms of y.**
   - Every explanation has a graph.
   - The mixed-form problems rewrite y = f(x) as x = g(y).
   - No problem supplies bounds the curves already define.
4. **Piecewise integrals.**
   - Type exact answers such as 40/3, 2π + 1 and 6 + 3 ln 2 with the keypad and with the keyboard.
   - Read the split-and-add explanation.
5. **Cross sections, non-calculator.**
   - Show Graph opens and closes the base.
   - The explanation's slice is perpendicular to the right axis.
   - The cross-section picture matches the shape named.
6. **Cross sections, calculator.**
   - No graph before Part A is graded.
   - Part A's explanation shows the base with a slice and the cross section.
7. **Solids, non-calculator.**
   - About half the questions show a graph, with the axis inside the frame and no stretched scale.
   - The explanation shows R and r, or the shell radius and height, beside the disk, washer or shell.
8. **Solids, calculator.**
   - No graph in the question.
   - Axes include the x- and y-axes, lines on each side and a boundary line.
   - Two-axis problems revolve the same region twice.
9. **Grading.**
   - Try a correct setup in another form (expanded, π inside, split, reversed with a minus sign): it is accepted.
   - Try an integral with the right value but the wrong integrand or limits: it is refused.
   - Where the prompt says either method or either variable, try the other one: it is accepted.
10. **Reviews.** The Practice Type menus read "Area & Volume — Multi-Part Practice" and "Graph Analysis & Accumulation —
    Multi-Part Practice".
11. **Phone.** Repeat a few of the above on a phone: graphs and pictures stack, nothing scrolls sideways.

## Limitations (stated plainly)
- **Grading**:
  - The checker recognizes the listed setups and their equivalent forms, not every correct setup. A setup rewritten
    by a substitution is refused, and so is one that mixes dx and dy pieces where the problem does not list that
    split. Prompts claim only what the checker accepts.
  - Regions without inverse functions (the cosine–parabola and log–parabola calculator regions, and the free-response
    region under k/(1 + x²)) have one usable method per axis. Their prompts name that method.
- **Display**:
  - In the solid pictures, the labels of a very short slice can crowd each other.
  - Calculator area problems show the compact graph in the question: always for the intersection problems, as in
    v12.9.A, and about half the time for the direction tasks. The teacher's no-graph rule was for calculator cross
    sections and solids.
- **Code left in place**:
  - The former multiple-choice piecewise generator stays in `ap-topic-generators.js`. No page uses it, but some older
    site QA tools still test it.
- **Deferred and separate work**:
  - MathLive and the collapse of Part A's explanation (above).
