## Confirmed problems and corrections

- **Choosing dx/dy:** the original Part A shows both complete integrals. For the reported `y=3x`, `y=x`, `x=2` region (deterministic seed 20), it reveals Part B's two y-integrals. Part A now explains vertical slicing and only its own `∫₀²(3x−x)dx`. Part B retains the full change at y=2, changed right boundary, common left boundary and complete two-integral sum. Both completed setups are compared in final feedback.
- **Both-direction Area tasks (non-calculator and calculator):** the shared bounds paragraph rewrites the opposite-direction boundaries before that setup is requested. Part A now derives only its chosen-direction endpoints, using the first/last actual region pieces; all original shared bounds prose is deferred to Part B. Approximate calculator intersections are labeled as approximate and the existing full-precision entry rules remain intact.
- **Washers:** original outer-radius feedback gives the inner-radius formula; inner-radius feedback is empty. New feedback derives each radius from its own boundary and the rotation axis. Final volume feedback retains both radii, the original integral and evaluation.
- **Shells:** original radius feedback gives the height formula; height feedback is empty. New feedback explains the perpendicular axis-to-slice distance first, then the top-minus-bottom/right-minus-left height. Final work remains complete.
- **Incorrect radius submissions:** old hints explicitly confirm that the entered formula is the next part's inner radius/height. New hints address the current measurement without identifying the future answer. The same expressions remain incorrect under the unchanged grader.
- **Graph justification pairs:** a full explanation of numerical inflection/extremum locations necessarily gives the subsequent choice's correct reasoning. The minimal redesign asks the generic criterion first, then the numerical location task. This teaches the criterion and leaves a meaningful graph-reading exercise. Full sign/direction arguments and model written justifications remain after the location answer. Unpaired numerical extrema keep their original full explanations. Choices, correct choice indices, graph parameters and mathematical answers are unchanged; graph problem IDs change because their ordered answer strings change.
